# Scopewright: first paid pilot kit
Status: internal working copy | October 2026 | ABSOL Technologies (proposed operating brand of ABSOL LLC)

## One-line offer
**Turn rough walkthrough notes into a clear, contractor-reviewed scope of work—without spending an evening writing it.**

## Target customer and pain
Residential remodelers, specialty contractors, restoration and handyman businesses that frequently convert short site notes into client-facing job scopes. Focus on owner-operators who have repeat paperwork and no dedicated estimator or administrative staff.

## Paid service pilot (proposed, not yet sold)
**Price to test:** $99 for a straightforward residential scope; $149–$199 for an involved multi-room scope after reviewing input. Quote custom/large jobs separately. These are hypotheses; confirm willingness to pay and actual delivery time. No subscription required.

**Customer supplies:** rough notes, optional photos (with appropriate permission), business letterhead details, exclusions, allowances, and project location at city level where relevant. Ask for missing dimensions and choices; never invent measurements or amounts.

**We deliver:** a structured editable/reviewable draft of the scope, inclusions, exclusions, allowance placeholders, and open questions; a printable version; one reasonable revision after contractor review. Contractor approves the scope and handles licensing, estimating, code, contractual obligations, and final signature.

**Data and privacy:** Current Scopewright software is local-first. A manual done-for-you service is different: the customer must choose to send notes to the operator. Obtain permission, minimize personal information, agree on retention/deletion, and do not upload private client materials into public GitHub. Avoid broad promises about encryption, retention, or compliance until operational practices are verified.

## Initial delivery workflow
1. Buyer sends de-identified or permissioned rough notes, project type, and known dimensions.
2. Review for gaps and ask clarifying questions where needed.
3. Run Scopewright on approved local machine.
4. Personally inspect every scope item against source notes; correct unsupported assumptions, especially under exclusions/open questions.
5. Send draft and revision instructions. Record the actual time needed and any corrections.
6. Collect customer feedback and ask whether they would pay again.

## Sample demo — fictitious bathroom walk (never publish as a real customer's project)
**Input notes:**
> Guest bath. Tub is staying. Remove loose tile surround and old drywall around tub, rebuild backing as needed after inspection, re-tile surround. Homeowner chooses tile later. Vanity might stay. Fan noisy and might need replacement; route TBD. Floor by doorway looks soft, investigate when opened. Paint walls after dust control. Include disposal. Verify measurements on next visit.

**Expected output checks (not a fabricated model output):**
- Scope clearly distinguishes tile demo, inspection, backing repairs *if authorized*, waterproofing system selection, tiling, painting, debris handling.
- Include visible TBD tile allowance; don't fabricate a tile brand, square footage, or dollar amount.
- Flag vanity stay/replace and fan scope/duct routing as open questions.
- Flag softness at doorway as concealed-conditions investigation and not assume structural repair is included.
- Explicit exclusions for hidden damage repairs beyond separately authorized scope, permits where applicable, and unseen electrical/ventilation modifications.
- Contractor-review gate prior to customer delivery.

## Discovery questions
- When a job walk ends, who writes the proposal scope and how long does it take?
- What often gets missed or rewritten after client feedback?
- Do allowances, scope changes, and exclusions cause disputes?
- Would you rather upload notes to a tool, install local software, or send them to a service?
- What would a contractor-reviewed scope be worth if it saved 45–90 minutes?

## Pilot success criteria
- Run 20 anonymized real-job evals and log the miss rate for critical unknowns.
- Three paid jobs and documented customer permission.
- Human review catches unsupported claims before delivery.
- Actual labor and cost recorded per pilot; margin positive after revisions.
- At least one customer indicates specific repeat-work interest.
