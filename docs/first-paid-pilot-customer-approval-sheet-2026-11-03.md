> **Contractor-facing summary, for approval:** Turn rough residential job-walk notes into an organized **draft scope of work** showing proposed inclusions, exclusions, selection/allowance placeholders, and unanswered questions. You review, correct, price and approve the final scope before it reaches your customer.

## 2. Consent and privacy check — complete BEFORE accepting job notes

- [ ] Contractor confirms they have authority to share the notes and any images with ABSOL LLC for this drafting service.
- [ ] Contractor removes customer names, full street addresses, phone numbers, access codes, signatures, payment details and unnecessary identifiers. **No identifiable client photos without appropriate permission.**
- [ ] Both sides agree in writing on the **actual processing workflow** (local software vs. any external services), transfer channel, access, retention/deletion date and any backups.
- [ ] Contractor understands that a done-for-you service **requires sharing notes with an operator**. It is not accurate to claim the notes never leave the contractor's device.
- [ ] Owner approves the final terms and checks insurance, business name and intellectual-property authority for the contemplated sale.

**STOP** if any of these remain unanswered. Do not accept real client notes, collect a fee, or promise a specific privacy/security standard without a workable documented process.

## 3. Fit screen — do not sell the first three pilots on high-risk jobs

**Good initial candidates:** simple interior paint, baseboards/trim, cosmetic bathroom refresh with no known hidden damage, or uncomplicated flooring replacement with verified conditions.

**Decline or require separate qualified review:** load-bearing/structural changes, deck or hot-tub capacity, electrical/service sizing, active water or mold damage, hazardous materials, unknown code/permit determinations, insurance claim guarantees, and emergency/unsafe work. Drafts can list unknowns; they cannot authorize or certify high-risk work.

## 4. Delivery and acceptance record — complete privately

1. Record agreed fee, deliverables, revision limit, due date and data handling before work starts.
2. Save original permissioned/de-identified notes in the agreed **private** location.
3. Generate and **line-check** the draft against those notes; do not silently fill gaps or invent dimensions, brands, quantities, prices or approvals.
4. Explicitly surface every material `TBD`, `?`, `verify`, `maybe` or concealed-condition uncertainty as an open question or clearly excluded decision.
5. Mark each output **DRAFT — CONTRACTOR REVIEW REQUIRED**. Contractor reviews and approves before sending to its customer.
6. Record any correction round, delivery date, actual operator time, direct costs, invoice and **cash received**. Honor the agreed data deletion process.

**A paid pilot counts only after actual payment is received from a distinct contractor** for an agreed Scopewright service. Interest, drafts, sent emails and invoices do not count as paid pilots.
