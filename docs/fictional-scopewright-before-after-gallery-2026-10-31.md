## Example A — Bathroom refresh: choices and concealed conditions

### BEFORE: fictional raw walkthrough notes

> Guest bath tub stays. Loose tile around tub, wall behind? tear out tile and bad board only. New surround tile owner picks later. Vanity maybe stays. Fan rattles, vent route unknown. Floor feels soft by door, check after demo. Paint walls. Haul trash. Verify measurements.

### AFTER: illustrative draft scope of work (contractor must review)

**Project intent:** Refresh existing guest bathroom finishes while keeping the tub in place. Final selections, dimensions, and concealed conditions remain unverified.

**Included in proposed base scope, subject to field verification and written approval**
1. Protect adjacent finished areas and set up dust-control measures appropriate to the agreed work.
2. Remove loose tub-surround tile and affected backing within the **field-confirmed removal limits**; document exposed conditions before proceeding with repairs.
3. Prepare an approved substrate and waterproofing approach **after the contractor confirms existing conditions and chosen system**; install customer-selected replacement surround tile once selected and authorized.
4. Prepare and paint agreed bathroom wall surfaces, excluding any surfaces not identified in the final work area.
5. Collect and remove debris generated by the agreed work.

**Selections / allowances — amounts NOT established**
- Surround tile: customer selection pending; no material brand, quantity, allowance dollar value, or lead time assumed.
- Replacement backing/waterproofing method: confirm after inspection; do not quote concealed repair work before findings.
- Vanity and exhaust fan: **not included in the base scope** until scope and routing decisions are confirmed.

**Explicit exclusions / separately authorized work**
- Structural/subfloor repairs or mold/water remediation behind finishes, unless documented and separately approved.
- Vanity replacement, fan replacement, new duct routing, electrical changes, plumbing relocation, and permits unless later added in writing.
- Concealed damage beyond agreed removal limits; additional work requires a documented change order.

**Open questions to resolve before final customer proposal**
1. What are the measured tub-surround and wall-paint limits?
2. Is the vanity staying? If not, who supplies and installs the replacement?
3. Is the fan being repaired or replaced, and where does the duct terminate?
4. What caused the soft flooring near the doorway? Is inspection-only authorized, and what happens if damage is found?
5. Which tile, pattern, trim and waterproofing system are approved? Who supplies materials?
6. Who determines permit requirements and obtains any necessary approvals?

**Contractor review gate:** Confirm every inclusion and exclusion, verify conditions, set pricing and schedule separately, and approve before giving this draft to a customer.

**Grounding check:** The notes contain **no** dimensions, brands, dollar amounts, authorization for structural repairs, or confirmed fan routing. None have been invented here.

## Example B — Deck repairs: structural and hot-tub risk

### BEFORE: fictional raw walkthrough notes

> Rear deck boards rough, some rot? rail wobbly, stairs squeak. Owner wants composite maybe. 2 posts look bad? no measurements. Hot tub might go on deck next year. Check joists, beams and footings. Permit? Snow load? want quote soon.

### AFTER: illustrative draft scope of work (contractor must review)

**Project intent:** Assess a deteriorating rear deck and develop a repair/resurfacing scope. Existing structural capacity and any future hot-tub loading are **unknown**.

**Included in proposed initial assessment scope**
1. Document accessible deck surface, stair and guard/handrail conditions, and visible framing/footing concerns.
2. Field-measure the deck, stairs, and railing; record visible rot and damage locations.
3. Prepare a **separate, conditional** repair/resurfacing proposal after determining whether the existing structure can be retained.

**Potential follow-on work — NOT YET INCLUDED OR PRICED**
- Remove and replace deck boards within measured, agreed limits.
- Repair stairs or guards only after specific defects, fastening details and requirements are determined.
- Replace or reinforce posts, beams, joists or footings only with appropriate design/permit review and written authorization.

**Selections / allowances — amounts NOT established**
- Composite vs. wood decking: customer decision pending.
- Railing/stair material and finish: not selected.
- Hidden framing damage: no assumed quantity or price.

**Explicit exclusions / separately authorized work**
- **No hot-tub placement or load-capacity certification.** Future hot-tub support requires a separately engineered/verified design and any necessary permits.
- No representation that the existing deck meets current code or local snow loads.
- No structural alteration, demolition, footing replacement, or permit application included in this assessment-only draft.

**Open questions**
1. What are the field measurements and current framing/post/footing sizes and condition?
2. Which guards and stairs are deficient, and what repair design is permitted?
3. Is composite actually selected, and who supplies it?
4. Will the owner require a separate engineer/permit review for future hot-tub loads?
5. What are the applicable local code and permitting requirements?

**Contractor review gate:** A qualified professional must verify structural design and permit needs; the contractor must approve a written repair scope before any work is sold.

**Grounding check:** No dimensions, structural capacities, post sizes, snow loads, engineering approval, or material brands were supplied; none are asserted.

## Example C — Leak-damaged flooring: moisture and hazard unknowns

### BEFORE: fictional raw walkthrough notes

> Kitchen sink leaked, plumber coming? floor buckled toward dining, maybe 2 rooms. Wet baseboard about 18ft? smells musty, could be mold. Tenant there. Insurance maybe. Need dry first. LVP replacement? owner deciding. Check moisture, subfloor, leak fixed?

### AFTER: illustrative draft scope of work (contractor must review)

**Project intent:** Determine whether the plumbing leak has been stopped and whether the affected rooms are ready for a separate flooring-restoration proposal. Extent of moisture damage and possible hazards are unknown.

**Included in proposed initial assessment scope**
1. Coordinate access with the authorized property contact and confirm the plumber's leak-repair status.
2. Document visible flooring and baseboard damage and **field-verify** the affected areas and measurements.
3. Identify whether additional qualified moisture/hazard assessment is required **before** proposing removal or replacement.

**Conditional future restoration — NOT YET INCLUDED OR PRICED**
- Remove and dispose of identified damaged finish flooring/baseboards after source control, safety assessment, and agreed work limits.
- Prepare substrate and install owner-approved replacement flooring **only when** drying/clearance requirements and subfloor condition are established.
- Reinstall/replace trim and perform agreed finish touch-ups.

**Selections / allowances — amounts NOT established**
- Replacement floor material: LVP is a possible preference, **not a confirmed selection**.
- Area/quantity: “about 18 ft?” and “maybe 2 rooms” are rough notes, not verified takeoff.
- Insurance participation: unconfirmed; no claim approval or reimbursement assumed.

**Explicit exclusions / separately authorized work**
- Leak repair, professional water mitigation/drying, mold testing/remediation, asbestos/lead testing, subfloor/structural repair, and electrical work unless separately scoped by appropriately qualified parties.
- No work in suspected hazardous conditions before assessment and required controls.
- No insurance coverage, code compliance, drying clearance, or restoration warranty claim implied.

**Open questions**
1. Has the leak been repaired and documented?
2. What are verified moisture readings, drying/clearance requirements and affected limits?
3. Is the musty odor associated with microbial growth or another condition needing specialist assessment?
4. Which rooms and measured areas are included? What is the subfloor condition?
5. Who authorizes work in the occupied unit, and what access/tenant protections apply?
6. What flooring product is approved and who pays? Is there a confirmed insurer scope?

**Contractor review gate:** Stop before destructive work if moisture/hazard status is unresolved; the responsible contractor and qualified specialists must review and authorize the final scope.

**Grounding check:** No mold diagnosis, drying status, floor area, claim approval, material price, or remediation authorization was supplied; none is invented.
